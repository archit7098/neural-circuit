# The Six Neural-Circuit Biotypes — Full Reference

Derived from *"Brain Circuits: Clinical Identification for Psychiatrists"*
(Williams Neural Circuit Taxonomy; Stanford Precision Mental Health; iSPOT-D).
For each circuit: neuroanatomy → pathophysiology → clinical phenotype →
non-radiological assessment → treatment implications.

Circuits are grouped by operational state:

- **Intrinsic (task-free)** — assessed at rest; symptoms color the whole
  baseline: **Default Mode, Salience, Attention**.
- **Task-evoked (regulatory)** — recruited on demand; dysfunction shows as a
  failed *reaction* to a stimulus: **Negative Affect, Positive Affect, Cognitive
  Control**.

---

## I. Default Mode Circuit — the "Rumination" Biotype (`DMN`)

**Neuroanatomy.** Posterior Cingulate Cortex (PCC) + Medial Prefrontal Cortex
(mPFC) midline core; Angular Gyrus + Precuneus lateral hubs. Supports
self-referential thought, autobiographical memory, future-envisioning, theory of
mind. Normally deactivates when an external task begins (Salience network signals
the switch).

**Pathophysiology.** Intrinsic **hyperconnectivity** — mPFC and PCC lock into a
hypersynchronous loop; the circuit becomes "sticky" and fails to deactivate.
Internal noise depletes resources for external tasks (interference, not a primary
capacity deficit).

**Clinical phenotype — the hallmark is Rumination.**
- Repetitive negative thinking: a "broken record"/"loop" the patient cannot
  voluntarily stop, focused on past regret or future catastrophe.
- **Initial insomnia** — trouble *falling* asleep because the mind races (a key
  discriminator).
- **Internal** distractibility — pulled *inward* (vs ADHD pulled outward); stares
  blankly, misses parts of conversations while analyzing what was just said.

**Non-radiological assessment.**
- Scales: **RRS** (Ruminative Responses Scale) high "Brooding"; **BRISC** high
  Negativity Bias + low Emotional Resilience.
- Proxy (WebNeuro/IntegNeuro): deficits in **immediate verbal recall** (from
  interference, not hippocampal damage); **slowed reaction time to sad faces**.

**Treatment.** Rumination-focused **CBT** or **Behavioral Activation**;
mindfulness-based cognitive therapy (disengagement). Pharma: SSRIs work but can be
sluggish; **Vortioxetine** may better address cognitive interference.
Neuromodulation: TMS to mPFC (inhibitory) or DLPFC (excitatory, to strengthen
top-down DMN suppression). ⚠️ Unstructured "supportive" talk can *reinforce* the
loop.

## II. Salience Circuit — the "Anxious Avoidance" Biotype (`SAL`)

**Neuroanatomy.** Anterior Insula (AI) + Dorsal Anterior Cingulate Cortex (dACC),
with connections to Amygdala and Striatum. The brain's sentinel: monitors
external and **interoceptive** input to decide what is salient; interrupts
cognition and toggles DMN↔executive networks on threat.

**Pathophysiology.** **Hyperactivity** and structural **expansion** (Lynch et
al. 2024: salience network ~2× larger in depression, stable, detectable in
at-risk children). Insula becomes hypersensitive to interoceptive signals; normal
fluctuations (skipped beat, gut tingle) are amplified into "danger"; the alarm
sounds but cannot be silenced → persistent autonomic arousal.

**Clinical phenotype — the hallmark is Somatic Anxiety + Anxious Avoidance
("in the body", not "in the head").**
- Visceral distress as the chief complaint: palpitations, chest tightness,
  stomach "knots", dizziness, sweating; hyper-aware of bodily function.
- Easily **overwhelmed / startled** by sensory input (lights, noise); broken
  "filter".
- **Avoidance** driven by intolerable *physical* sensation (not anhedonia).

**Non-radiological assessment.**
- Scales: **GAD-7 / DASS-21** high on **somatic** items ("I could feel my heart
  beating"); **BRISC** high Negativity Bias with intact resilience (over-detecting
  threat).
- Proxy: **slowed reaction to threat faces** — "frozen"/overwhelmed (contrast
  with Negative Affect, which is *fast*).
- Physical: high resting HR / reduced HRV.

**Treatment.** **Exposure & Response Prevention**, especially **interoceptive
exposure**; MBSR. Pharma: **beta-blockers** (propranolol) or alpha-2 agonists
(clonidine/guanfacine) to break the body↔insula loop; SSRIs to lower system gain.

## III. Negative Affect Circuit — the "Threat" Biotype (`NEG`)

**Neuroanatomy.** Amygdala, Hippocampus, Anterior Insula, Subgenual Anterior
Cingulate (sgACC / BA25). The survival circuit: rapid (often nonconscious) threat
detection, defensive behavior, negative-memory encoding, the *feeling* of sadness.

**Pathophysiology.** **Hyperactivation** of amygdala + sgACC to negative stimuli;
"trigger-happy" amygdala fires at subliminal threat; weak top-down PFC inhibition,
but the primary driver is the **bottom-up** limbic storm. Can activate
nonconsciously (masked faces) → unease without a known cause.

**Clinical phenotype — the hallmark is Negative Cognitive Bias + mood
congruency.**
- **Intense** misery/dysphoria — sadness, guilt, fear, irritability (contrast
  with the *numbness* of Positive Affect).
- **Catastrophizing** — neutral/ambiguous events read as negative ("dark-tinted
  glasses").
- **Rejection sensitivity**; hypersensitive to criticism.
- Strong link to **Early Life Trauma** / chronic stress (amygdala sensitization).

**Non-radiological assessment.**
- Scales: **CTQ** (Childhood Trauma Questionnaire) high; **BRISC** high Negativity
  Bias (the defining metric).
- Proxy (the gold-standard proxy): **Implicit Threat Bias** task — **faster**
  reaction to angry faces (vigilance) or **misidentifying neutral faces as
  angry/sad**.

**Treatment.** Classic **SSRI responder** (sertraline, escitalopram — serotonin
downregulates amygdala firing); good first-line response. Psychotherapy:
**Cognitive Restructuring (CBT)** for automatic negative thoughts; add
emotion-regulation / trauma processing (EMDR, Schema Therapy) since bias is often
implicit.

## IV. Positive Affect Circuit — the "Reward" Biotype (`POS`)

**Neuroanatomy.** Ventral Striatum (Nucleus Accumbens), Ventral Tegmental Area
(VTA, dopamine source), Orbitofrontal Cortex (OFC). Governs reward, motivation,
pleasure; the engine of approach behavior. Two phases: **anticipatory** ("wanting"
— dopamine) and **consummatory** ("liking" — opioids/endocannabinoids).

**Pathophysiology.** **Hypoactivation** of striatum + VTA; blunted reward
prediction-error signal → no drive to engage.

**Clinical phenotype — the hallmark is Anhedonia + Apathy.**
- "The blahs": **flat / numb / empty** rather than explicitly sad; "I don't care
  about anything."
- Loss of motivation, psychomotor retardation; stops hobbies because they
  anticipate **no pleasure** (not anxiety, not rumination).
- Social withdrawal (interaction offers no reward signal).

**Non-radiological assessment.**
- Interview cue: *"If I told you that you won the lottery today, would you feel
  excited?"* → often "No." "Do you force yourself to do things you used to love
  but feel nothing?"
- Scales: **SHAPS** (Snaith-Hamilton Pleasure Scale) high; **DARS** (Dimensional
  Anhedonia Rating Scale) to separate wanting vs liking.
- Proxy: reward tasks show **no reaction-time speed-up** when money is at stake;
  **low accuracy for happy faces**.

**Treatment.** ⚠️ **SSRI-resistant** — serotonin can inhibit striatal dopamine
(5HT2C) and *worsen* anhedonia. Prefer dopaminergic/noradrenergic agents:
**Bupropion** (NDRI), **Pramipexole** (dopamine agonist; Williams et al. showed
efficacy when SSRIs fail), augmentation with low-dose aripiprazole/brexpiprazole.
Psychotherapy: **Behavioral Activation focused on savoring / reward exposure
(Positive Affect Treatment, PAT)** — not mere activity scheduling.

## V. Attention Circuit — the "Inattention" Biotype (`ATT`)

**Neuroanatomy.** Fronto-Parietal Network: Dorsolateral Prefrontal Cortex
(DLPFC), Inferior Parietal Lobule (IPL), Anterior Cingulate. Sustains the
"spotlight" of attention; working memory; vigilance.

**Pathophysiology.** **Hypoconnectivity** between frontal and parietal nodes
(especially at rest); the spotlight can't be held → focus lapses. In MDD often
state-dependent cognitive slowing (overlaps with, but is distinct from,
developmental ADHD).

**Clinical phenotype — the hallmark is Inattention + Lethargy + Cognitive
Slowing.**
- "I read the same paragraph five times"; "I drift off in meetings."
- Appears tired, slow, "checked out."
- ⚠️ **Poor initial response to talk therapy** — the attentional channel is
  closed, so the "dose" of CBT never lands.

**Non-radiological assessment.**
- Interview cue: "Can you sit through a movie?" "Do you zone out even when trying
  to listen?"
- Psychometrics: **Digit Span** (forward/backward) poor.
- Proxy: **Continuous Performance Test / Oddball** — **errors of omission**
  (missing targets because attention drifted; distinct from impulsivity).

**Treatment.** **Sequencing matters — medication first** to restore attentional
capacity *before* psychotherapy can work. Pharma: **SNRIs** (venlafaxine,
duloxetine) or noradrenergic agents over pure SSRIs; stimulants
(lisdexamfetamine/methylphenidate) as adjuncts. **Cognitive remediation** to
strengthen the fronto-parietal link.

## VI. Cognitive Control Circuit — the "Cognitive Dyscontrol" Biotype (`COG`)

**Neuroanatomy.** DLPFC + dACC — the brain's executive. "Top-down" control:
suppresses the amygdala (calms Negative Affect) and the DMN (stops rumination);
enables goal-directed behavior and inhibition of prepotent responses.

**Pathophysiology.** The "cognitive biotype" (Williams et al. 2024: ~27% of
depressed patients). **Hypoactivation** (failure to recruit) or maladaptive
**hyperactivation** (inefficient effort) of DLPFC/dACC on cognitive tasks — the
brain's "brakes" fail.

**Clinical phenotype — the hallmark is Brain Fog + Executive Dysfunction +
Impulsivity.**
- Decision paralysis ("I can't decide what to eat"); "I feel foggy."
- Emotional dysregulation ("I know I shouldn't be angry/sad but I can't stop it")
  — no brakes on mood.
- Cognitive rigidity — trouble switching tasks/adapting plans.

**Non-radiological assessment.**
- Scales: **BRISC** low Emotional Resilience + low Social Skills (coping).
- Proxy (the diagnostic key): **Go/NoGo** — **errors of commission** (pressing on
  NoGo; can't inhibit). **Stroop** — high **interference** (can't suppress the
  automatic reading response).

**Treatment.** ⚠️ **Least likely to respond to SSRIs** (remission ~38% vs ~47%);
identifying this biotype prevents months of futile SSRI trials. Prefer
**Venlafaxine (SNRI)**, **Vortioxetine**, or **Bupropion** (noradrenergic/
dopaminergic boost recruits DLPFC). **Prime candidate for TMS** targeting the
DLPFC. Psychotherapy: **Problem-Solving Therapy (PST)** — concrete structured
skills, since abstract CBT can overshoot their executive deficits.

## Quick differential tie-breakers

| If the patient... | It points to... | Not... |
|---|---|---|
| Is distracted by their **own thoughts** (inward) | Default Mode | Attention (outward/drift) |
| Feels anxiety **in the body** and avoids | Salience | Negative Affect (in the mood) |
| Reacts to threat faces **fast** (vigilant) / neutral=angry | Negative Affect | Salience (slowed/frozen) |
| Feels **numb/empty**, no motivation | Positive Affect | Negative Affect (intense misery) |
| **Misses** targets, lethargic, can't focus | Attention | Cognitive Control |
| **Can't inhibit** — impulsive, brain fog, can't decide | Cognitive Control | Attention |
